Understanding Your Prescription (Rx)

A medical prescription is a formal clinical order written by a licensed healthcare professional. Understanding its components ensures you receive and take the right medication.

  • Rx Symbol: Derived from the Latin recipe ("take thou"). Signifies the start of the therapeutic instruction.
  • Medication & Strength: Indicates the active chemical agent and the milligram (mg) or milliliter (ml) strength per unit dose.
  • Frequency Abbreviations: Common medical notations:
    • OD: Once daily
    • BD / BID: Twice daily (every 12 hours)
    • TDS / TID: Three times daily (every 8 hours)
    • QID: Four times daily (every 6 hours)
    • SOS / PRN: Take only as needed for symptoms
  • Relation to Meals: AC means before food; PC means after food.
🌡

Safe Storage & Environmental Factors

Improper temperature and moisture degrade pharmaceutical stability and reduce medication effectiveness.

  • Cool & Dry Place: Store oral tablets and capsules away from direct sunlight, excess heat, and bathroom moisture. Keep under 25°C.
  • Refrigeration (2°C – 8°C): Insulin pens, unopened vaccines, and certain eye drops must be kept in the main compartment of your refrigerator. Never freeze medicines.
  • Keep Out of Reach of Children: Always store medicines in secure cabinets with child-resistant caps firmly tightened.
  • Original Packaging: Retain medicines in their original blister packs or bottles to protect from moisture and preserve expiry batch details.
⚕

Responsible Antibiotic Stewardship

Antibiotics treat bacterial infections, not viral illnesses like common colds or influenza.

  • Finish the Full Prescribed Course: Even if your symptoms improve after two or three days, stopping early allows surviving bacteria to develop resistance.
  • Never Share Antibiotics: Never use leftover antibiotics prescribed for someone else or a previous illness.
  • Adhere to Timed Intervals: Take antibiotics at consistent hours (e.g. every 8 hours for TDS) to maintain steady therapeutic blood levels.
♻

Expiration Dates & Safe Disposal

Expired medicines can lose potency or break down into harmful sub-compounds.

  • Checking Expiration Dates: The date stamped on the strip or bottle indicates the final day of that month the manufacturer guarantees safety and efficacy.
  • Do Not Flush Down Drains: Flushing antibiotics and hormonal drugs contaminates water supplies and the environment.
  • Hospital Take-Back Bins: Drop expired or unused medicines at the Metropolitan Hospital Pharmacy safe-disposal receptacle for certified biomedical incineration.

5 Important Questions to Ask Your Pharmacist

1. What is the exact purpose of this medicine?

Understand which condition or symptom each medication addresses.

2. How and when should I take it?

Confirm timing relative to meals and whether it induces drowsiness.

3. What foods, drinks, or supplements should I avoid?

Certain juices, dairy, and herbal products interfere with absorption.

4. What should I do if I accidentally miss a dose?

Never take a double dose without specific pharmacist instructions.
